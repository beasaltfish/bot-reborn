# BME280 breakout selected for the simulator

The physical reference is the Adafruit BME280 STEMMA QT breakout, product 2652,
not an unspecified four-pin BME280 module. The manufacturer lists dimensions of
25.2 × 18.0 × 4.6 mm. The seven 2.54 mm header positions, left to right when
viewed as in the manufacturer guide, are VIN, 3Vo, GND, SCK, SDO, SDI, CS.
The extra STEMMA QT connectors are not yet modeled as connection points.

For I²C, SCK is SCL and SDI is SDA. VIN accepts 3–5 V and should match the
controller's logic voltage; the breakout has a 3.3 V regulator and level
shifting. 3Vo is a regulated output, not a second supply input. The default
address is 0x77; closing the ADDR jumper or connecting SDO to GND selects
0x76. This simulator's address setting represents the solder jumper. A
wiring helper resolves SDO connected to the module's ground and feeds shared
resource and sensor evaluation; other SDO/CS connections remain unsupported
rather than assumed safe.

The manufacturer's [PCB schematic](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-BME280-Breakout-PCB/blob/master/Adafruit%20BME280.sch)
shows 10 kΩ pull-ups on both sides of the SDA/SCL level shifters; the header-side
pull-ups connect to VIN. Thus VIN at 5 V can pull the header bus toward 5 V even
when the controller uses 3.3 V logic. The simulator does not yet calculate
parallel pull-ups, line current, or rise times and must not claim signal safety
from connectivity alone.

The Bosch chip's datasheet current figures are chip-only and operating-mode
dependent; they are not a measured module-wide idle/peak budget. Do not treat
them as a complete breakout current model. Shared evaluation supports a bounded direct-3.3-V I²C topology. The selected
workbench sensor exposes simulated readings and session-only environment controls;
the lab offers a separate example and a five-stage lesson. Broader electrical
topologies remain in development, and no current real firmware support is claimed.
