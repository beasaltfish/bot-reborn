Audio-chain spike

Throwaway verification code, not on web/'s product path (but kept in the repo). No LLM, no car. One getUserMedia feeds both KWS and VAD; a segment the VAD cuts goes to STT, and the transcript is read back by TTS. It answers five questions: whether a combined VAD build works at all, what KWS and VAD cost together, transcription quality on a real phone mic (⑩), TTS on a code-switched sentence (⑪), and whether barge-in is possible here at all (§7.3).

Barge-in and self-triggering (what §7.3 rests on)

While TTS is playing, the pipeline keeps feeding KWS and VAD but does not act on what they conclude — it only counts. Two things have to hold, and neither on its own is enough: the robot's own voice must not trigger the VAD, and your voice must still get through. A single mixed window contains three sources — echo that survived the canceller, you, and the room — and cannot separate those two questions: talking over it as instructed scores as echo leaking in, and staying quiet scores as a pass on the strength of echo alone. So the self-test runs to a script, and the screen tells you when to speak. The rows below only mean anything compared with each other; the absolute dB values are useless.

Verdict—

A level says whether your voice gets through, not whether what got through is still intelligible. Cancelling echo usually takes a bite out of the speech along with it — enough for the VAD to still clear its threshold while STT starts dropping characters. So the same sentence is read twice and both readings go to STT: ② is the control, recorded in silence, and ⑤ is the one with the robot talking next to you. Without the control, a bad transcript cannot be told apart from a microphone that was simply bad to begin with.

Keep-alive sound (measuring how far it lifts the noise floor)

spike/kws/ established that playing something very quiet while the screen is off keeps the microphone, with all three clocks running to the end. The price is an amplifier that never powers down, which lifts the noise floor of the room — and the noise floor is row ① of the barge-in verdict, last measured at −75.5 dB without any keep-alive.
Turn the keep-alive on here, run the barge-in self-test again, and compare rows ① and ④ to see how much headroom it ate. This page only plays the sound; it does not do the keeping-alive (the self-test runs with the screen on throughout, so nothing needs keeping alive).

Parameters

One keyword per line. They are validated against tokens.txt before Start — a wrong token aborts the wasm module outright.
