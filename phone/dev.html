② Byte rate — what one ms of driving costs

What comes out is bytesPerMs: how many bytes buy one millisecond of the pins being held. Six runs in two passes — the byte counts tell a rate from a truncated write, the bauds tell it from a chip that ignores what it was asked. The verdict is checked against what ftdi.js uses. ② says what the chip delivers; this says what the car does with it. Below some length the motor gets current and the wheels never break loose. Tap a length, watch the car, then tap ✓ or ✗ — ten tries each, and the answer is the smallest pulse that moves it every single time. why it has teeth

    LLM

    It has to answer and to call a tool. Those are two different things, verified separately.

    TTS

    Synthesises the check line for the language the robot is set to — code-switched in Chinese, plain in English. Every language in it has to be intelligible.

    Reflex (JEV)

    Optional. Recognises somebody barking at the robot before the LLM is asked. Leave empty to turn it off.

    USB

    Connect first; the typed driver needs it too. The test writes the shortest pulse the robot itself will issue — green only means the bytes reached the chip. Nothing moving is not proof of bad wiring: what actually starts this motor is ⑧ on the pin bench, still unmeasured.
